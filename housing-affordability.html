About these numbers

  • House prices: REINZ median sale price for August each year, for today's regional council areas. Figures before 2017 are from interest.co.nz's REINZ series.
  • Incomes: Stats NZ median weekly household income from all sources before tax, June year, times 52 (Household Labour Force Survey). It covers households with at least one person aged 18 to 64, so retiree-only households are left out. That suits a measure about buying a home, but it runs higher than Stats NZ's all-household figure.
  • Interest rates: Reserve Bank average 1-year fixed rate for new borrowers, August. Special rates (the discounted rates most buyers get) are only recorded from 2017. Standard rates go back to 2005 but are higher than most people paid.
  • Deposit and loan: the deposit is 20% of the price unless you choose 10%. Repayments are principal and interest on the rest of the price over 30 years (or 25), compared with income before tax so years stay comparable.
  • Income needed is the household income before tax at which repayments on the median house would take the benchmark share you've chosen. 30% of income is a common rule of thumb for housing costs. In most regions the median house has needed more than that in recent years, so choose the share you'd consider manageable.
  • Averages are simple averages of the years you've chosen. For repayments on special rates they start in 2017, when the special rate figures begin.
  • Six smaller regions aren't shown because their data is too thin: Gisborne, Hawke's Bay, Nelson, Tasman, Marlborough and the West Coast. The national line covers all of New Zealand.
